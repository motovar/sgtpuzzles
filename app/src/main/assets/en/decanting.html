Decanting

A puzzle that is better known as “water sort”. Glass tubes hold layers of coloured liquid, and each tube has room for the same number of layers. Your objective is to sort the liquids so that every tube is either empty or full of a single colour: all the layers of one colour belong in one tube.

How to pour

A move pours the liquid from the top of one tube into another tube. The liquid on top of a tube is the layers of the colour at the top: if a tube holds (from the bottom) red, blue, blue, then the top is the two blue layers. You may pour into a tube if

  • it is empty, or its top layer has the same colour as the layers that you pour, and
  • it has room for at least one more layer.

All the layers of the top colour move across if they fit; if the other tube has room for only some of them, as many as fit move across and the others stay where they are. A tube is never poured into itself, a tube that is empty cannot be poured from, and liquids of different colours do not mix: a layer can go only on to a layer of the same colour.

You can undo every pour, so you can try things out. Some games have only one or two tubes more than there are colours, and then it is easy to pour yourself into a corner where no solution is left; Show next step (see below) tells you so, and then you have to undo some moves.

What you see

The tubes stand in a row (or in two rows, when there are a lot of them), with the layers of each of them from the bottom up. The status bar counts your moves (“Moves”); when the puzzle is solved the tubes flash and the status bar says COMPLETED!. It also tells you if there is no pour possible at all.

The colours are chosen to be easy to tell apart. If you find some of them hard to distinguish, switch on the labels in the preferences (Labels on the layers): every layer then carries the number or the letter of its colour.

Hidden layers

With the parameter Hide the layers below the top only the layers on top of a tube can be seen: of every tube you see the layers of the colour at the top, and a grey layer with a question mark for each of the others. When you pour and the layers below come to light, they are shown, and they stay shown even if you cover them again by pouring on top of them, so you can remember what you have found out; so it is a game of exploring as well as of sorting. (Show next step and Solve know all of the layers, hidden ones included, and use them.)

Controls

You pour in two taps:

  • Tap a tube to select it: it is lifted up and its glass turns blue. (An empty tube cannot be selected.)
  • Tap another tube, and the liquid is poured into it if that is possible. If it is not possible, the tube that you tapped is the selected one now (unless it is empty), so you can pick another tube to pour from without an extra tap.
  • Tap the selected tube again, tap outside the tubes or press and hold to let go of the selection.

The arrow buttons of the app and the cursor keys of a keyboard move the cursor (a thin blue frame around a tube) from tube to tube: left and right go through the tubes one after the other, up and down go from one row to the other when there are two rows. The first press of a key only shows where the cursor is. The Select button (Enter on a keyboard) selects the tube that the cursor is on, or pours into it from the tube that is selected; Space on a keyboard lets go of the selection.

Show next step, Solve

Show next step works out a solution from the position that is on the screen and makes its first pour for you. It is an ordinary move, which you can undo, and it takes care not to lead you round in circles: it tries to avoid the positions that the game has been in before. Solve makes all the pours of a solution at once, starting from the position on the screen; Undo takes them all back in a single step.

The solver looks for short solutions (they are not always the very shortest ones). When no solution can be found from the position you are in, you are told so, and you have to undo some moves yourself.

Decanting parameters

These parameters are available from the ‘Custom…’ option on the ‘Type’ menu.

Colours
The number of colours: from 2 to 12.
Tubes
The number of tubes: at most 16, and more than there are colours. The tubes that are not needed to hold the colours start empty: they are the room that you have to work with. The fewer of them there are, the harder the game is: with just one empty tube it is easy to make a move after which the game cannot be finished any more.
Layers
How many layers a tube holds: from 2 to 8. Every colour has exactly as many layers as that, so a colour fills a tube.
Hide the layers below the top
Switches on the hidden layers that are described above.

The presets are 4 colours in 6 tubes, 7 colours in 9 tubes and 10 colours in 12 tubes (all of them with four layers per tube and two empty tubes), 12 colours in 14 tubes of 5 layers, a harder game with 7 colours in 8 tubes (a single empty tube), and 7 colours in 9 tubes with hidden layers.

The layers are dealt out at random, and only games that can be finished are kept. Where it takes too long to find out whether a game that has been dealt out can be finished (few empty tubes, many layers), the game is made by scrambling a finished one with pours that run backwards. Such a game can always be finished, too; it tends to be a little shorter.

Decanting preferences

Labels on the layers
None (the default), numbers or letters: what is written on the layers to help tell the colours apart.

Status

This puzzle is playable. The idea and the first version of the game (the tubes, the selection and the pouring) are by Lalo Martins, from his fork of the puzzle collection. In this app there is also a game generator that makes sure that games can be finished, a solver for Show next step and Solve, hidden layers, a complete check of the game description and of every move, a count of the moves, labels on the layers, and the cursor keys.
