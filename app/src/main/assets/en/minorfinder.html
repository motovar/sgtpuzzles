Minor Finder

A puzzle about graphs. You are shown a big graph (blue points joined by lines) and a small one, the minor (red points). Your objective is to turn the big graph into the small one, using the three operations that mathematicians use to make a minor of a graph: you may contract an edge, delete an edge and delete a point. When the big graph has become the small one, with the same number of points joined in the same way, the puzzle is solved. (It does not matter where the points are on the screen, only which points are joined.)

The three moves

  • Contract an edge: the two points at the ends of the edge are merged into one point. The new point has all the edges that either of the two had (if both were joined to the same point, that is still a single edge), and the edge between them is gone.
  • Delete an edge: the edge is removed; its points stay.
  • Delete a point: the point is removed together with all the edges that end in it.

Moving a point to another place is not a move in this sense: it changes nothing about the graph, and you can use it as often as you like to make the picture clearer (it can be undone, like everything else).

Every game can be solved from the beginning. A way to think about it: every red point stands for a group of blue points that hang together. Contract the edges inside each group until the group is a single point; delete the points that belong to no group, and the edges that the small graph does not have. The groups have to be chosen so that two of them are joined by an edge whenever the two red points are.

It is easy to get stuck by deleting something that was needed, or by contracting an edge that should not have been contracted. Show next step (see below) tells you if the position that you are in cannot be solved any more, and then you have to undo some moves.

What you see

The small graph (the minor) is drawn in a square of its own, above the big graph or beside it, as you like (see the preferences). The big graph is the one that you play with. The status bar tells you how many points and edges the big graph has now and how many it has to get down to, for example “Points: 9 (need 4)   Edges: 14 (need 5)”. (Both numbers being right does not mean that you have won: the points have to be joined in the right way, too.) When the puzzle is solved the board flashes and the status bar says COMPLETED!; if the game was solved for you, it says Auto-solved.

While you are playing, some things are coloured:

  • a green point is the one that you are holding or have tapped;
  • when a point has been tapped, its neighbours are marked by a light blue halo: those are the points that it can be merged into;
  • a point that you are dragging over one of its neighbours makes that neighbour bigger: let go, and the two are merged;
  • a point or an edge that is going to be deleted when you let go is orange (with a point, all of its edges turn orange too).

Controls

The puzzle is played with the finger or the mouse; there is no keyboard control.

  • Move a point: drag it where you want it. (A very short drag counts as a tap, below.) A point can not be put on top of another point or into the border of the board; if you let go there nothing happens.
  • Contract an edge: either drag one of its points on to the other one (the point that you drop on gets bigger to show that the two will be merged; the point that you drag goes into it), or tap one of the two points and then the other (the first one goes into the second one).
  • Delete a point or an edge: press and hold on it (with a mouse: press the right button). The point or edge turns orange; let go while the finger or the pointer is still on it to delete it, or slide away from it before you let go to leave it alone. If a point and an edge are both near, the point wins.
  • Let go of a selection: tap the point that you tapped before, or tap beside all the points. Tapping another point that is not a neighbour of the one that you tapped moves the selection to the new point.
  • Dragging off the screen cancels the drag.

The points are easy to hit with a finger: you do not have to be exactly on the point. Edges are not as easy, and a long press next to a crowd of lines may pick a different edge than the one that you meant: the orange edge shows which one it is before anything is deleted.

Show next step, Solve

Show next step works out a way to the small graph from the position on the screen and makes its first move for you: first it deletes the points that no group needs, then it contracts the edges inside the groups, then it deletes the edges that are too many. It is an ordinary move, which you can undo; you can press the button again and again to be led to the end. If you play a move of your own meanwhile, the next step is worked out for the new position.

Solve makes all of the moves at once and also puts the points that are left on the places of the points of the small graph; Undo takes it all back in one step.

The solver is exact: if there is a way from the position on the screen it finds one (it does not take your own earlier choices into account, only the graph that you have now), and if there is none, it says so: “This position cannot be solved; undo some moves”. A search for a way is given a generous limit of effort; in a rare, very hard position it may run out of it and say “No solution was found in time”. Undoing a few moves, or making a few that you are sure about (contract an edge, delete a point that is no use), makes the search easier.

Minor Finder parameters

These parameters are available from the ‘Custom…’ option on the ‘Type’ menu.

Minor
“Random graph” (the default): the small graph is a connected graph chosen at random. “K5 or K3,3”: the small graph is the complete graph on five points (K5) or the complete bipartite graph on three plus three points (K3,3). A graph can be drawn on a flat sheet without crossing lines exactly when it has neither of these two as a minor (Wagner’s theorem), so in these games you are showing, move by move, that the big graph cannot be drawn without crossings.
Points of the big graph
From 15 to 23 (from 19 to 23 for K5 and K3,3). More points, more edges and more to choose from.
Points of the minor
From 4 to 6. With K5 or K3,3 it has to be 5 for the K5 and 6 for the K3,3.

The presets are the big graph with 15 points and a minor with 4, 19 and 5, 23 and 6, and the two with K5 (19 points) and K3,3 (23 points).

The games are made by constructing the big graph around the small one, so that a way always exists; the generator also checks the finished game with the solver, to be sure.

Minor Finder preferences

Position of the minor
“Above the big graph” (the default, good for a phone held upright) or “Beside the big graph” (good for a wide screen). In both cases the big graph is as large as the screen allows.

Status

This puzzle is playable. The game, and the way that its games are generated, are by Samuel Holderbach (“Minorensucher”, from his bachelor thesis). In this app there is also an exact solver for Show next step and Solve, a generator that checks that every game can be solved (and that no longer fails for numbers of points that are a multiple of the size of the minor), a complete check of the game description and of every move, touch controls (tapping two points, long press to delete, big targets), a status line and the choice of where the minor is shown.
