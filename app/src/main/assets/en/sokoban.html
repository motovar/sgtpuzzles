Sokoban

Push all the barrels into the target squares

You are the warehouse keeper. Push the barrels around the maze so that all of them end up on the target squares. You can only push a barrel, never pull it, and you cannot push two barrels at once.

This puzzle comes from the "unfinished" folder of Simon Tatham's collection. It uses a simple random level generator rather than the hand-made levels of the classic game.

Sokoban controls

Tap next to the keeper (above, below, left or right, or diagonally) to take a step in that direction; if a barrel is in the way it is pushed. Diagonal steps only move the keeper and are allowed when one of the two squares on the way is free. The arrow pad can be shown from the menu. Undo and redo are available as usual.

Sokoban parameters

Width, Height
Size of the warehouse.

Status

Fully playable. The generated levels are always solvable. The next-step button shows the next move of a solution when one can be found (it gives up on big levels).

Level generators

The presets use the original generator. Under Custom you can choose another one. The four alternative generators run the code of their authors; what was added around them is only what a puzzle on a phone needs: the size of the board, time limits, and the filling of unreachable floor with wall.

  • Original: the generator that ships with the puzzle (random reverse play). It makes four candidates, keeps the one that uses the most floor and opens up some of the unused floor next to it (this tweak was added to the app earlier, the reverse-play code itself is the upstream one).
  • Boulders (NetHack style): the level maker of miki151/sokoban: a row of pits has to be filled with boulders. As in the original app it makes randomised attempts for a time budget (5 seconds) and keeps the deepest level. The original board is 28x16 with 4 boulders (3 to 7 can be chosen there); the board here has the size you set (at least 10x6).
  • MCTS: the Monte Carlo tree search generator by lkseg (based on the paper "Data Driven Sokoban Puzzle Generation with Monte Carlo Tree Search"), its source files unchanged apart from the console output being silenced. As in the original app it is given the width and height of the board, which may have 16 to 254 squares, and the hardest level of the search is used. Unreachable floor is filled with wall.
  • YASGen: YASGen 1.50 by Brian Damgaard (the level generator of Sokoban YASC), translated to C function by function: the same genetic algorithm, the same fitness and the same settings as the generator window of YASC. It runs for a number of seconds (the original runs until you stop it) and with a smaller table of positions (48 MB, the original 128 MB).
  • Rooms: the port of smartive/rust-sokoban-level-generator (after Taylor & Parberry): rooms are assembled from 17 rotated 3x3 templates until a layout meets the requirements, goals are placed at random and the position farthest from the solved state is found by a reverse-move search. The number of tries for a layout and the number of positions the search records are limited (the original has no limit).

Big boards: the alternative generators give good puzzles only on boards of modest size: up to 254 squares for MCTS (as in the original app), 28x16 for Boulders, 14x14 for YASGen and for Rooms (4x4 rooms; a layout of more rooms practically never meets the requirements). A bigger board is therefore built from several such levels, laid out in a grid and separated by one square wide streets; the player solves them one after the other, each tile being linked to the next by a tunnel and a corridor. The number of boxes asked for in the Custom dialog (YASGen, Rooms) is the total for the whole board; for Boulders it is per structure.

You can move by swiping on the board: a short swipe moves one square, and if you keep dragging on, the player keeps walking (one square for each longer stretch of the drag). The arrow buttons are larger for Sokoban. Graphics themes (Original, Classic, four Yoshi skins) are chosen in the game preferences.

The Custom dialog shows the settings of the selected generator, and switches to those of another generator as soon as you pick it. Boulders: number of boulders (0 = 4), search time in seconds (0 = 5) and search nodes per attempt (500). MCTS: search time (0 = automatic), how many of the best levels to keep, and which of them to play (1 = hardest, the number of levels to keep = easiest). YASGen: search direction (backward from the goals, or forward), maximum number of boxes (0 = 4), interior walls in percent, time limit, goals at random or connected, fitness (number of pushes, or box lines and box changes), whether trivial opening pushes are discarded, and the tail pushes threshold. Rooms: number of boxes, search limit (thousand positions) and the number of goal placements to try.

In the game preferences, Graphics selects the original look or a Classic look with bricks and crates.
