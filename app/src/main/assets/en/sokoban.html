Sokoban

Push all the barrels into the target squares

You are the warehouse keeper. Push the barrels around the maze so that all of them end up on the target squares. You can only push a barrel, never pull it, and you cannot push two barrels at once.

This puzzle comes from the "unfinished" folder of Simon Tatham's collection. It uses a simple random level generator rather than the hand-made levels of the classic game.

Sokoban controls

Tap next to the keeper (above, below, left or right, or diagonally) to take a step in that direction; if a barrel is in the way it is pushed. Diagonal steps only move the keeper and are allowed when one of the two squares on the way is free. The arrow pad can be shown from the menu. Undo and redo are available as usual.

Sokoban parameters

Width, Height
Size of the warehouse.

Status

Fully playable. The generated levels are always solvable. The next-step button shows the next move of a solution when one can be found (it gives up on big levels).

Level generators

The presets use the original generator. Under Custom you can choose another one:

  • Original: the generator that ships with the puzzle (random reverse play).
  • Boulders (NetHack style): a row of pits has to be filled with boulders to open the way to the exit; a port of the generator from miki151/sokoban.
  • MCTS: Monte Carlo tree search generator by lkseg (based on the paper "Data Driven Sokoban Puzzle Generation with Monte Carlo Tree Search"). Unreachable bits of floor are filled with wall.
  • YASGen: Brian Damgaard's YASGen0 (from YASC, GPL): a genetic algorithm evolves small rooms (walls, goals, player) and uses YASGen's exhaustive reverse search as fitness (longest solutions). Settings: boxes, wall density, search limit, generations.
  • Rooms: port of smartive/rust-sokoban-level-generator (Apache-2.0, after Taylor & Parberry): the level is assembled from 3x3 rooms (17 rotated templates), goals are placed at random and the position farthest from the solved state is found by a reverse-move search. Any number of boxes can be asked for.

Big boards: these four generators make good puzzles only on boards of modest size (about 14x14 for MCTS, 16x16 for YASGen, 17x17 for Rooms, 26x13 for Boulders). A bigger board is therefore built from several such levels, laid out in a grid and separated by one square wide streets; the player solves them one after the other, each tile being linked to the next by a tunnel and a corridor. The number of boxes asked for in the Custom dialog (YASGen, Rooms) is the total for the whole board; for Boulders it is per structure.

You can move by swiping on the board: a short swipe moves one square, and if you keep dragging on, the player keeps walking (one square for each longer stretch of the drag). The arrow buttons are larger for Sokoban. Graphics themes (Original, Classic, four Yoshi skins) are chosen in the game preferences.

The Custom dialog shows the settings of the selected generator, and switches to those of another generator as soon as you pick it. Boulders: number of boulders, attempts (more attempts give a deeper level) and search nodes per attempt. MCTS: search time, how many of the best levels to keep, and which of them to play (1 = hardest, the number of levels to keep = easiest). YASGen: reverse or forward mode, number of goals, wall density, search limit, number of genetic generations and random player start. Rooms: number of boxes, search limit and the number of goal placements to try.

In the game preferences, Graphics selects the original look or a Classic look with bricks and crates.
