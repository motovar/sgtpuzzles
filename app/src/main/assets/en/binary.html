Binary

A sliding game with numbered tiles that is better known as “2048”. The board holds tiles that carry powers of two: 2, 4, 8, 16 and so on. Every move slides all the tiles as far as they will go in one of the four directions. When two tiles with the same number run into each other they merge into one tile with the sum of the two (2 and 2 make 4, 4 and 4 make 8, and so on), and after every move a new tile – a 2, and now and then a 4 – turns up on a random empty square.

Your objective is to make a tile with the goal number, which is 2048 unless you choose another goal. When you have done that you can go on playing to build even bigger tiles; the game is over when the board is full and no two neighbouring tiles are equal, so that no move is left.

The details of a move

  • A tile that has been made by a merge does not merge again in the same move: a row of 2 2 4 8 slid to the left gives 4 4 8, not 16.
  • When there are three equal tiles in a row, the two that are nearest to the side you slide to merge: 2 2 2 slid to the left gives 4 2.
  • A move that would not change anything (no tile can slide, no tiles can merge) is not a move, and no new tile turns up after it. You cannot make one.
  • Undo cannot be used to roll the dice again: every game carries the random numbers it needs, so if you undo a move and play the same move again, the same tile turns up on the same square.

What you see

Every number has its own colour: pale tiles for 2 and 4, orange and red for 8 to 64, golden yellow for 128 to 2048, and green, teal, blue and purple for the tiles beyond that. Squares without a tile are shown as faint slots (or in the style that you choose under Game background, see below).

The status bar shows the goal and your score. The score goes up by the number of every tile that a merge makes. WON! is added when the goal tile has been made and NO MORE MOVES when the game is over; the tiles of a game that is over turn grey.

Controls

There are two ways to move the tiles with your finger. You choose the one you prefer in the preferences (Input method):

  • Swipe (the default): drag your finger across the board, about half a tile or more, mainly up, down, left or right. All the tiles slide that way when you lift your finger. A short or diagonal drag does nothing, so you can change your mind by going back to where you started.
  • Tap: the two diagonals of the board divide it into four triangles, one at every side. A tap on the triangle that is at the top slides the tiles up; the triangles on the left, right and bottom work the same way.

The arrow buttons of the app and the cursor keys of a keyboard slide the tiles, too.

Show next step

There is no way to “solve” a game of this kind, as nobody knows what tiles turn up. Show next step looks ahead instead: it works out the four moves, the new tiles that may turn up after each of them and the moves after that, rates the boards that can come out of that (by the number of free squares, the tiles that can merge and how orderly the rows and columns are) and plays the move that is best on average. The move is an ordinary one, so you can undo it. On the normal sizes the hint plays a game through to the goal; it tells you when there is no move left.

Binary parameters

These parameters are available from the ‘Custom…’ option on the ‘Type’ menu.

Width, Height
Size of the board in squares: from 2 to 9 in both directions. The presets are 4x4 with the goal 512, 4x4, 5x5 and 6x6 with the goal 2048.
Goal
The number that you have to make: 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096 or 8192. Choose a goal that suits the size of the board: on a very small board a high goal cannot be reached, and the game ends with no move left.

Binary preferences

Game background
How the squares without a tile look: faint slots (the default), plain empty squares, a chessboard or a grid of lines.
Input method
Swipe or Tap, as described above.

Status

This puzzle is playable. It is the game “2048” by Gabriele Cirulli; this implementation comes from the collection PocketPuzzles by Steffen Bauer. In this app the tiles merge once per move, as in the game that it is modelled on (the first version of the code let a merge go on in a chain), and there is a hint.
