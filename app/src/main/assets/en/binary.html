Binary

Binary is a sliding game with numbered tiles. It has two sets of rules: “2048”, the game that it is best known as, and “Threes”, the game that 2048 was made after. You choose the rules, and for 2048 the goal, under Type; the presets are listed below.

2048

The board holds tiles that carry powers of two: 2, 4, 8, 16 and so on. Every move slides all the tiles as far as they will go in one of the four directions. When two tiles with the same number run into each other they merge into one tile with the sum of the two (2 and 2 make 4, 4 and 4 make 8, and so on), and after every move a new tile – a 2, and now and then a 4 – turns up on a random empty square. The numbers are generated as the original game does it: two tiles at the start, then after every move that changed the board one tile that is a 2 nine times in ten and a 4 the tenth time, on an empty square that is picked at random (every empty square is as likely as the others).

Your objective is to make a tile with the goal number, which is 2048 unless you choose another goal. When you have done that you can go on playing to build even bigger tiles; the game is over when the board is full and no two neighbouring tiles are equal, so that no move is left.

With the goal Endless there is nothing to win: you play for as long as you can and for as many points as you can, until no move is left. (The biggest tile there can be is 131072, and the game is over when one has been made.)

The details of a move

  • A tile that has been made by a merge does not merge again in the same move: a row of 2 2 4 8 slid to the left gives 4 4 8, not 16.
  • When there are three equal tiles in a row, the two that are nearest to the side you slide to merge: 2 2 2 slid to the left gives 4 2.
  • A move that would not change anything (no tile can slide, no tiles can merge) is not a move, and no new tile turns up after it. You cannot make one.
  • Undo cannot be used to roll the dice again: every game carries the random numbers it needs, so if you undo a move and play the same move again, the same tile turns up on the same square.

Threes

The tiles are 1 (blue), 2 (red) and then the white ones: 3, 6, 12, 24 and so on, each one the double of the one before. A move does not send the tiles as far as they will go but pushes them one square in the direction that you slide. A tile moves into an empty square, or on to a tile that it can merge with:

  • a 1 and a 2 merge into a 3, and
  • two equal tiles of 3 or more merge into one with the double number (3 and 3 make 6, 6 and 6 make 12 and so on).

Two 1s or two 2s do not merge, and neither do tiles that are different otherwise. In a row (or column) that moves, the tiles that are behind the first tile that can move or merge all move one square together with it; a row where nothing can move or merge stays as it is. So a row 3 3 3 3 slid to the left gives 6 3 3 and an empty square at the end, and a row with a gap, such as – 3 3 –, only closes the gap (3 3 – –) and the two 3s merge with the next move.

The game starts with nine tiles on the board. After every move that changes the board a new tile comes in at the far end of one of the rows (or columns) that have moved, a row picked at random. The strip above the board shows what the next tile will be: a blue 1, a red 2, a white 3, or a + for a bonus tile. The new tiles come out of a bag with four 1s, four 2s and four 3s, so that you get as many of the three kinds as you need over a time; when the bag is empty it is filled again. When the biggest tile is 48 or more, now and then (one time in 21) the next tile is a bonus tile instead, a white tile with a number from 6 up to an eighth of the biggest tile.

There is no goal in Threes: you play on until no move is left. The score is the one of the real game: a tile of 3 or more is worth 3 to the power of the number of times it has been doubled plus one, which is 3 points for a 3, 9 for a 6, 27 for a 12, 81 for a 24 and so on; the 1s and 2s are worth nothing. The status bar shows the score and the biggest tile.

What you see

The two games look like the games that they are made after.

2048 is on the cream page of the original, on a brown board with rounded corners and squares for the tiles. The tiles have the colours of the original: pale for 2 and 4, orange and red for 8 to 64, and yellow for 128 to 2048, where the tiles from 128 on glow with a blue haze that gets stronger up to 2048; all the tiles beyond 2048 are dark. A game that is over is covered by a milky veil, as in the original. Squares without a tile are shown as faint squares (or in the style that you choose under Game background, see below).

Threes has upright cards with round corners and a darker edge below, on a light blue screen: the 1 is blue, the 2 is red and all the other tiles are white. The empty places are darker hollows, and above the board a small card shows the tile that comes next (blue, red or white, with a + for a bonus tile). The cards of the real game also have faces, which are the artwork of that game and are not drawn here; the numbers tell the tiles apart.

The status bar shows the goal (or Endless, or Threes), your score and, in the games without a goal, the biggest tile. The score of 2048 goes up by the number of every tile that a merge makes. WON! is added when the goal tile has been made and NO MORE MOVES when the game is over.

Animation

In 2048 the tiles slide to their new squares in a tenth of a second; then the tile that a merge has made pops up over the two that have merged, and the new tile grows where it turns up – the timing and the curves of the original page. In Threes all the tiles of the rows that move slide one square, and the new card slides in from beyond the edge, together, in a fifth of a second. Undo and redo run the same movement backwards and forwards. You can make it faster, or turn it off, with the preference Animation; a move that you make while the picture is still moving takes over at once.

Controls

There are two ways to move the tiles with your finger. You choose the one you prefer in the preferences (Input method):

  • Swipe (the default): drag your finger across the board, about half a tile or more, mainly up, down, left or right. All the tiles move that way when you lift your finger. A short or diagonal drag does nothing, so you can change your mind by going back to where you started.
  • Tap: the two diagonals of the board divide it into four triangles, one at every side. A tap on the triangle that is at the top slides the tiles up; the triangles on the left, right and bottom work the same way.

The arrow buttons of the app and the cursor keys of a keyboard move the tiles, too.

Show next step

There is no way to “solve” a game of this kind, as nobody knows what tiles turn up. Show next step looks ahead instead: it works out the four moves, the new tiles that may turn up after each of them and the moves after that, rates the boards that can come out of that and plays the move that is best on average. The move is an ordinary one, so you can undo it. It tells you when there is no move left.

  • In 2048 the boards are rated by the number of free squares, the tiles that can merge and how orderly the rows and columns are. On the normal sizes the hint plays a game through to the goal.
  • In Threes it knows the tile that is shown as the next one, and takes the tiles after that to be a 1, a 2 or a 3 with the same chance. It rates the boards by the free squares, the pairs of neighbours that can merge, how orderly the lines are and the number of 1s and 2s, which are in the way until a 1 and a 2 meet. Playing a 4x4 board on it, it lasts several hundred moves and makes tiles of 384 and more, where moves at random end after fewer than fifty.

Binary parameters

These parameters are available from the ‘Custom…’ option on the ‘Type’ menu.

Width, Height
Size of the board in squares: from 2 to 9 in both directions.
Rules
2048 or Threes, as described above.
Goal
For 2048, the number that you have to make: 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096 or 8192, or Endless for no goal. Choose a goal that suits the size of the board: on a very small board a high goal cannot be reached, and the game ends with no move left. Threes has no goal, so what you choose here does not matter for it.

The presets are 4x4 with the goal 512, and 4x4, 5x5 and 6x6 with the goal 2048; 4x4 and 5x5 Endless; and Threes on a 4x4 and on a 5x5 board. (The real Threes is played on 4x4.)

Binary preferences

Game background
How the squares without a tile look: faint slots (the default), plain empty squares, a chessboard or a grid of lines.
Input method
Swipe or Tap, as described above.
Animation
Normal (the default), Fast or Off.

Status

This puzzle is playable. 2048 is the game by Gabriele Cirulli; this implementation comes from the collection PocketPuzzles by Steffen Bauer. In this app the tiles merge once per move, as in the game that it is modelled on (the first version of the code let a merge go on in a chain), the numbers are generated and the game looks and moves like the original, and there is a hint, the goal Endless and the rules of Threes (the game by Asher Vollmer, Greg Wohlwend and Jimmy Hinson; this is a version of its rules written for this app, with the bag, the bonus tiles and the score as players have worked them out of the real game, and its colours and shapes but not its artwork).
