Maze 3D

Find your way through a maze that is three-dimensional – so don't forget to look up and down too! You stand inside the maze and see the passage in front of you in perspective; you can look up, down, left and right, roll your head sideways, and walk forwards or backwards along the passage you are looking down. Your objective is to reach the target, which shows up as a yellow disc at the end of a passage when you can see it.

What you see

The big square on the left is the view along the passage you are facing: the walls, the floor and the ceiling of every cell you can see (up to eight cells ahead) are drawn in perspective, and a passage that is open to a side is drawn as an opening. The colour of a wall depends on the compass direction it faces, so you can keep your bearings even when you have rolled over: green for north and south, pink for east and west, blue for up and down. Cells that you have marked are outlined in pink.

The panel on the right shows

  • how many steps (Moves) and how many turns (Turns) you have made so far, and how many markers you have dropped (Marks);
  • the size of the maze;
  • depending on the Display setting, either nothing, your coordinates in the maze, or the distance to the target as the crow flies and the compass direction it lies in;
  • a compass: for each of the six directions (up, down, left, right, forwards and backwards, from where you stand) the letter of the compass direction it points to – N, S, W, E, U (up, towards the ceiling of the whole maze) or D (down).

Controls

Touch the picture:

  • the middle walks one cell forwards (if there is no wall in the way);
  • the top and bottom edges turn your view up and down, the left and right edges turn it left and right;
  • the corners roll you: the top right and bottom left corners roll clockwise, the top left and bottom right corners roll anticlockwise;
  • long press drops a marker in the cell you are in, or takes it away again. The markers are only there to help you remember where you have been.

The buttons below the puzzle have the same moves and some more: Fwd and Back walk forwards and backwards, Turn turns you right around, Roll L and Roll R roll you, and Mark drops or takes away a marker.

With a keyboard: the cursor keys (or 8, 2, 4 and 6) turn up, down, left and right; Enter, Space, + or 5 walk forwards; B or − walk backwards; T or * turn around; A, 7 or 3 roll anticlockwise; C, 9 or 1 roll clockwise; M or 0 drop or take away a marker.

When you stand on the target the puzzle is complete.

Solve and Show next step

For your first few games you may find it helpful to play on a flat maze (height 1), or to ask for help:

  • Solve does not walk for you. It shows a yellow thread that leads from where you are to the target (and the direction to take next lights up in yellow in the compass); using it again hides the thread.
  • Show next step makes the next move along a shortest way to the target for you: it turns you towards the way to go, or walks one cell. It is a move like any other, so you can undo it.

Maze 3D parameters

These parameters are available from the ‘Custom…’ option on the ‘Type’ menu.

Width, Length, Height
Size of the maze in cells, from 2 (but at least 3 x 2 x 1 in all) to 20.
Target location
Where the target can be: in a Corner of the maze, on an Edge, on a Face of the maze, or Anywhere. You start at the place that is furthest from the target by way of the passages.
Double Routes/Circles
If on, some of the walls are knocked down after the maze has been made, so that there are several ways to the target and passages that lead round in circles. This makes the maze much harder.
Display
What the panel shows below the size: Nothing, Coordinates, or Direction/Distance to the target.

Status

This puzzle is playable. There are no difficulty settings; the size, the place of the target, the double routes and what is displayed are the settings that make a maze harder or easier.

Maze 3D was written by Edward Macnaghten (2006) and is part of the collection of puzzles for handhelds by Lee Dowling (stppc2x).
