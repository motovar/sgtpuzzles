Mosco

A puzzle from Ivan Moscovich's “Magic Arrow Tile Puzzles”. You are given a rectangular arena that is to be filled up completely with arrow tiles, and a ring of numbers around it. Every number counts the arrows that point at the square it stands in – along the row, the column or the diagonal that the square is on, no matter how far away the arrow is and no matter what lies in between. Your objective is to give every tile of the arena an arrow so that every number is right.

There is one more rule: you have a fixed set of tiles to use. Each of the eight kinds of arrow (up, up-left, left, down-left, down, down-right, right and up-right) is used exactly area / 8 times – two of each kind on a 4 x 4 arena, eight of each on 8 x 8. This is why the area of the arena has to be a multiple of eight.

There are usually several arrangements that fit the numbers, and any of them completes the puzzle.

What you see

The numbers around the arena are black while they are still to be fulfilled, green when exactly that many arrows point at them, and red when too many arrows point at them already. The four corner numbers count along the diagonal only.

The arrows are coloured by the way they point: blue for up and down, green for up-left and down-right, red for left and right and yellow for up-right and down-left. When you have laid more tiles of one kind than there are in the set, those tiles get a red frame.

The status bar shows how many tiles of each of the eight kinds are still left to lay – they have to come out at zero all along when the arena is full.

Controls

Touch a tile of the arena:

  • a tap changes the arrow of the tile: it goes round from nothing to up, up-left, left, down-left, down, down-right, right and up-right, and back to nothing;
  • a swipe from the tile in the direction that you want lays that arrow straight away (a preview of the arrow shows while your finger is on the way, and you can still change your mind by swiping somewhere else before you let go);
  • a long press followed by a swipe puts a small “maybe” arrow in the corner or edge of the tile that lies in that direction, or takes it away again. These marks are only notes of your own; they do not count as arrows. A long press on one of the edges or corners of a tile (without swiping) switches the mark of that edge or corner, and a long press on the middle of the tile clears it of all arrows and marks.

A tap on a number highlights all the tiles that can point at it (and a second tap puts the highlight out), which makes it easy to see where its arrows can come from.

With a keyboard (or the cursor keys of your device): the cursor keys move the cursor over the arena and the numbers around it; Enter or Space change the arrow of the tile that the cursor is on (or highlight the number); the keys 7 8 9 / 4 6 / 1 2 3 of the number pad lay the arrow that points that way (8 is up, 6 is right, 7 is up-left and so on); 5, 0, Backspace and the second select key clear the tile.

When every number is right and every kind of arrow has been laid the right number of times, the puzzle is complete.

Solve and Show next step

  • Solve lays an arrangement that fits all the numbers on every tile of the arena, replacing the arrows that you have laid. It is the arrangement that the puzzle was made from or, for a game that was typed in, one that the program finds.
  • Show next step works out whether the arrows that you have laid can still be completed to a solution. If they can, it lays one more arrow for you – one that has to be there, when the numbers leave no choice, or otherwise one that goes with a way of completing the puzzle. If they cannot, it tells you which tile the mistake is on (it shows the row and the column of the arrow that stands in the way). The step is a move like any other, so you can undo it.

Mosco parameters

These parameters are available from the ‘Custom…’ option on the ‘Type’ menu.

Width, Height
Size of the arena in tiles. Both have to be at least 4 and at most 255 and the area (width times height) has to be a multiple of eight. The presets are 4x4, 6x4, 8x4, 8x8 and 16x16.

Status

This puzzle is playable. There are no difficulty settings: the size of the arena is the way to make the puzzle harder or easier. The numbers do not always pin down a single arrangement – that is how the puzzle is designed – so on the larger sizes the hint may have to choose one of several arrows that all lead to a solution.

The puzzle is from Ivan Moscovich's books “Magic Arrow Tile Puzzles”; Mosco is part of the collection of puzzles for handhelds by Lee Dowling (stppc2x).
