Mosco

A puzzle from Ivan Moscovich's “Magic Arrow Tile Puzzles”. You are given a rectangular arena that is to be filled up completely with arrow tiles, and a ring of numbers around it. Every number counts the arrows that point at the square it stands in – along the row, the column or the diagonal that the square is on, no matter how far away the arrow is and no matter what lies in between. Your objective is to give every tile of the arena an arrow so that every number is right.

There is one more rule: you have a fixed set of tiles to use. Each of the eight kinds of arrow (up, up-left, left, down-left, down, down-right, right and up-right) is used exactly area / 8 times – two of each kind on a 4 x 4 arena, eight of each on 8 x 8. This is why the area of the arena has to be a multiple of eight.

Every puzzle that the program makes has exactly one arrangement that fits, and you can always get to it by reasoning – no guessing is needed. How much reasoning it takes is what the three levels of difficulty are about (see below). When you type in a game description of your own, it may have more than one solution; any arrangement that fits the numbers and the set of tiles completes the puzzle then.

What you see

The numbers around the arena are black while they are still to be fulfilled, green when exactly that many arrows point at them, and red when too many arrows point at them already. The four corner numbers count along the diagonal only.

The arrows are coloured by the way they point: blue for up and down, green for up-left and down-right, red for left and right and yellow for up-right and down-left. When you have laid more tiles of one kind than there are in the set, those tiles get a red frame.

The status bar shows how many tiles of each of the eight kinds are still left to lay – they have to come out at zero all along when the arena is full.

Controls

Touch a tile of the arena:

  • a tap changes the arrow of the tile: it goes round from nothing to up, up-left, left, down-left, down, down-right, right and up-right, and back to nothing;
  • a swipe from the tile in the direction that you want lays that arrow straight away (a preview of the arrow shows while your finger is on the way, and you can still change your mind by swiping somewhere else before you let go);
  • a long press followed by a swipe puts a small “maybe” arrow in the corner or edge of the tile that lies in that direction, or takes it away again. These marks are only notes of your own; they do not count as arrows. A long press on one of the edges or corners of a tile (without swiping) switches the mark of that edge or corner, and a long press on the middle of the tile clears it of all arrows and marks.

A tap on a number highlights all the tiles that can point at it (and a second tap puts the highlight out), which makes it easy to see where its arrows can come from.

With a keyboard (or the cursor keys of your device): the cursor keys move the cursor over the arena and the numbers around it; Enter or Space change the arrow of the tile that the cursor is on (or highlight the number); the keys 7 8 9 / 4 6 / 1 2 3 of the number pad lay the arrow that points that way (8 is up, 6 is right, 7 is up-left and so on); 5, 0, Backspace and the second select key clear the tile.

When every number is right and every kind of arrow has been laid the right number of times, the puzzle is complete.

Difficulty levels

The levels differ in the reasoning that is needed to solve the puzzle (and in how flat the numbers are: the harder the level, the fewer big numbers). Every tile is to be told from the numbers; at no level is a guess needed.

Easy
Every tile starts with all eight arrows possible. A number that has all its arrows already rules out every other tile for that square, and a kind of arrow that has been used up rules out that arrow for all the other tiles; a tile that has just one possible arrow left has it, and a number that wants as many arrows as there are tiles that can still point at it gets them all.
Normal
As Easy, and a kind of arrow that wants as many tiles as can still have that arrow gets all of them – the count of the kinds (area / 8 of each) has to be used as well. Easy is not enough for a Normal puzzle.
Hard
As Normal, and where that stops you have to ask “what if”: a tile of which all but one of the possible arrows lead to a contradiction (by the rules above) has the one that is left. Normal is not enough for a Hard puzzle.

Not all of a big board is hard: a board of more than 72 tiles is made from a long chain of Easy steps and one small patch – about 24 tiles for Normal and Hard – that needs the reasoning of its level. Up to 72 tiles the whole board is a puzzle of its level. (A board that is just two tiles wide, or high, and a hundred tiles or more in size cannot be made harder than Easy by this: the program then makes the next easier level that works, and you will notice that the puzzle goes quickly.)

Solve and Show next step

  • Solve lays the solution on every tile of the arena, replacing the arrows that you have laid. It is the one arrangement that fits (for a game that was typed in, an arrangement that the program finds).
  • Show next step checks the arrows that you have laid against the solution. If one of them is wrong it tells you: either that too many arrows point at a number (or that too many tiles have one kind of arrow), which you can see yourself, or which tile the mistake is on (it shows the row and the column of the arrow that stands in the way). If everything laid is right, it lays one more arrow for you – one that follows from the numbers and the arrows that are laid already (a tile that has just one possible arrow left) or, where that doesn't go any further, the one that a “what if” shows to be the only one. The step is a move like any other, so you can undo it.

Mosco parameters

These parameters are available from the ‘Custom…’ option on the ‘Type’ menu.

Width, Height
Size of the arena in tiles. Both have to be at least 2 and the area (width times height) has to be a multiple of eight. (A single row or column can't make a puzzle with just one solution: the arrows that point up and those that point down, or left and right, could always be changed round without any number noticing.) There is no other limit than the memory: up to a million tiles are allowed. The numbers around the arena get bigger with the size of it, and the game writes them smaller as they get more digits. The presets are 4x4, 6x4, 8x4 and 8x8, each in the three levels.
Difficulty
Easy, Normal or Hard; see above. A big board takes longer to make than a small one, the more so the harder the level; it is a matter of seconds, not minutes.

Status

This puzzle is playable, with three levels of difficulty, a puzzle that has just one solution at every size and a hint that follows the reasoning of the levels.

The puzzle is from Ivan Moscovich's books “Magic Arrow Tile Puzzles”; Mosco is part of the collection of puzzles for handhelds by Lee Dowling (stppc2x).
